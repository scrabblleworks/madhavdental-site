Tooth Extractions & Oral Surgery

When a tooth cannot be predictably saved, Dr. Priti explains why removal may be needed and what happens next.

Madhav Dental Clinic · Kalkaji

Extraction is considered after the tooth has been assessed.

Dr. Priti's approach is to understand the condition of the tooth before deciding on removal. If a tooth can be treated and restored predictably, preservation is considered. When extraction is the appropriate option, the procedure and replacement choices are explained clearly.

01

Why an extraction may be needed

A tooth may be too damaged by decay, fracture or disease to restore reliably. Impacted or problematic wisdom teeth may also require removal.

02

What happens first

Dr. Priti examines the tooth and relevant dental findings before discussing whether treatment, restoration or removal makes the most sense.

03

What comes after

If a tooth is removed, the next decision may be whether the space should be replaced with a bridge, denture or implant, depending on the case.

Common Questions

What patients usually want to know

Will the extraction hurt?

The area is normally numbed before the procedure. Some soreness and swelling afterwards are possible, and Dr. Priti will give you after-care instructions suited to the procedure.

Can a tooth be saved instead?

Sometimes. The decision depends on the amount of healthy tooth remaining, the condition of the roots and surrounding tissues, and whether the tooth can be restored predictably.

When does a broken tooth need to be removed?

A broken tooth does not automatically need extraction. The decision depends on where and how much the tooth has fractured and whether enough healthy tooth remains to restore it.

Do all wisdom teeth need to be removed?

No. Wisdom teeth may be left alone when they are healthy, functional and can be kept clean. Removal may be recommended when they cause problems or cannot be maintained properly.

How long does an extraction take?

The time varies with the tooth and the complexity of the procedure. Dr. Priti can explain what is expected after examining the tooth.

What should I expect afterwards?

Some discomfort, swelling or minor bleeding can occur after an extraction. The socket needs time to heal, and the after-care instructions should be followed carefully.

What can I eat after an extraction?

For the early healing period, softer foods are usually easier. Avoid disturbing the extraction site and follow the specific after-care instructions provided by the clinic.

What if bleeding continues?

Some oozing is expected. If bleeding is heavy or does not settle with the instructions given by the clinic, contact the clinic promptly.

When should I contact the clinic?

Persistent or heavy bleeding, increasing severe pain, marked swelling, fever or other symptoms that concern you should be discussed promptly with the clinic.

Do I need to replace a missing tooth?

Not every missing tooth has the same implications. Dr. Priti can assess the space, the neighbouring teeth and your overall bite before discussing whether replacement would be useful.

Can an extracted tooth be replaced with an implant?

An implant may be one option, but it is not automatically the right choice. A bridge or denture may also be appropriate depending on the individual case.

What affects the cost of an extraction?

Cost depends on the tooth, the complexity of removal and whether additional treatment or replacement is needed. The clinic can explain the cost after assessment.

Been advised to have a tooth removed?

Speak with Dr. Priti before deciding what comes next.
