General & Preventive Dental Care

Good dental care is often about finding small problems before they become bigger ones.

General dental care covers the everyday work of keeping teeth and gums healthy, checking for problems and treating them before they become more difficult to manage.

At Madhav Dental Clinic, the starting point is an examination and a clear understanding of what is actually going on. The aim is not to recommend treatment simply because you have come for a check-up. It is to identify what needs attention, what can be watched, and what you can do to keep your teeth healthy.

01

Check what is happening

Teeth, gums and the surrounding tissues are examined for signs of decay, gum problems, worn or damaged teeth and other concerns.

02

Treat what needs attention

Depending on the finding, treatment may include cleaning, scaling, fillings or other dental care. More complex problems can be assessed and referred within the appropriate treatment plan.

03

Keep problems from building up

Good daily cleaning, sensible follow-up and regular review can help keep problems from being ignored until they become painful or difficult to treat.

Common care

Everyday dental problems need the right level of treatment.

Not every problem needs extensive treatment. The appropriate response depends on what is found during examination.

Dental check-ups

A check-up looks for problems you may not notice yourself, including early decay, gum inflammation, worn teeth and other changes.

Scaling & cleaning

Professional cleaning can remove plaque and hardened deposits that cannot be removed adequately with routine brushing.

Fillings

When decay has created a cavity, a filling may restore the affected tooth. The material and extent of treatment depend on the tooth and the size of the defect.

Gum care

Bleeding, swelling, persistent bad breath or gum recession can need assessment. Treatment depends on the cause and severity.

Tooth pain

A toothache can have different causes. Examination helps distinguish problems that may settle with simple care from those needing restorative or root canal treatment.

Early treatment

Finding a problem while it is still manageable can sometimes mean simpler treatment and a better chance of preserving the natural tooth.

Common Questions

Before you decide what to do

These are common questions. Your own treatment depends on the condition of your teeth and gums.

How often should I have a dental check-up?

The right interval depends on your teeth, gums, previous dental problems and risk factors. A dentist can recommend a recall interval after examining your mouth.

Do I need a cleaning if I brush every day?

Daily brushing and cleaning between the teeth are essential, but some people still develop hardened deposits or tartar that cannot be removed with a toothbrush.

Do cavities always need a filling?

It depends on the size and stage of the decay. Early changes may sometimes be managed with preventive care, while a cavity may require a filling or more extensive treatment.

Can a small toothache go away on its own?

Pain can settle temporarily without the underlying problem being resolved. A recurring or unexplained toothache should be assessed.

What should I do if my gums bleed when I brush?

Bleeding gums can be associated with gum inflammation, but other causes are possible. Persistent bleeding should be assessed.

Can regular dental visits prevent root canals or extractions?

Regular examinations can help identify problems earlier, when treatment may be simpler. They cannot prevent every root canal or extraction.

How long does a routine dental visit take?

It depends on what needs to be checked or treated. A routine examination may be relatively brief, while cleaning, fillings or other treatment can require additional time or appointments.

How much does a dental check-up cost?

The cost depends on what is included in the visit and whether any treatment is needed. The clinic can confirm the applicable charges when you book.

Start with a proper examination.

If something has been bothering you, or it has simply been a while since your last dental check-up, the first step is to find out what is actually happening.
