Full Mouth Rehabilitation

When several dental problems need attention, treatment works better when the whole mouth is planned together.

Full mouth rehabilitation is not one particular procedure. It is a coordinated treatment plan for patients whose teeth, gums, bite or ability to chew have been affected by several problems.

The first step at Madhav Dental Clinic is to understand what needs treatment, what can be preserved and what can safely be left alone. The aim is a sensible sequence of care rather than treating everything at once.

01

Assess the whole mouth

Teeth, gums, existing restorations, bite and areas of missing or damaged teeth are considered together.

02

Prioritise what matters

Pain, infection, damaged teeth and active disease are addressed before longer-term restorative decisions.

03

Build a sequence

Treatment is arranged in stages so that each step supports the next and the overall plan remains manageable.

What may be involved

There is no standard full-mouth plan.

The treatment depends on what is actually found during the assessment.

Repair and preserve

Fillings, root canal treatment, crowns and other restorative work may be used where a tooth can be retained and restored.

Replace what is missing

Bridges, dentures or dental implants may be considered where teeth cannot be saved or are already missing.

Improve function

Where tooth wear, bite problems or multiple restorations affect chewing, the plan takes the way the teeth work together into account.

A sensible treatment sequence

Not everything has to happen at once.

A comprehensive plan does not mean every procedure is carried out immediately. The order matters.

Stabilise

Address pain, infection, active decay and gum problems that need attention first.

Save what can be saved

Where possible, preserve useful natural teeth rather than removing them simply to simplify the plan.

Restore

Complete the appropriate fillings, root canal treatment, crowns or other restorations based on the agreed plan.

Replace and maintain

Replace missing teeth where needed and establish a practical maintenance routine for the finished result.

Common Questions

What patients usually want to know

What is full mouth rehabilitation?

It is a planned course of dental treatment for a patient who has several problems affecting the teeth, gums, bite or ability to chew. It may involve more than one type of restorative or preventive treatment.

Who may need full mouth rehabilitation?

It may be considered when there are multiple damaged, worn, missing or heavily restored teeth, significant bite problems, or several dental issues that need to be addressed as part of one overall plan. An examination is needed to determine whether this approach is appropriate.

Does full mouth rehabilitation mean every tooth needs treatment?

No. The aim is not to treat every tooth simply because a comprehensive plan is being made. Teeth that are healthy or can be maintained may not need restorative treatment.

What treatments can be part of full mouth rehabilitation?

Depending on the case, treatment may include cleaning and gum care, fillings, root canal treatment, crowns, bridges, tooth replacement or dentures. The combination is based on the patient's actual needs.

How long does full mouth rehabilitation take?

There is no single treatment time. It depends on the number and type of problems, the order in which treatment needs to be done and whether healing or laboratory work is required between stages.

How much does full mouth rehabilitation cost?

The cost varies substantially because full mouth rehabilitation is not one fixed procedure. It depends on which teeth need treatment, which restorations are required and whether tooth replacement or other procedures are involved. A useful estimate requires an examination and treatment plan.

Is full mouth rehabilitation painful?

The individual procedures are planned and carried out with appropriate local anaesthesia where needed. Some treatments can cause soreness or sensitivity afterwards, but the amount of discomfort depends on what is being done and how extensive the treatment is.

Will I need root canal treatment on all my teeth?

No. Root canal treatment is used when a tooth has an infected or damaged pulp and needs it. A full mouth plan does not mean every tooth automatically needs a root canal.

Can full mouth rehabilitation include dental implants?

It can, when tooth replacement is needed and implants are appropriate for the individual case. Implants are only one possible part of a full mouth rehabilitation plan.

Can my natural teeth be saved?

That depends on the condition of each tooth. A proper assessment should identify teeth that can be preserved and restored before decisions are made about extraction or replacement.

How is the treatment planned?

The dentist first assesses the teeth, gums, bite and existing dental work, then decides what needs attention and in what order. The final plan depends on the findings rather than following one fixed sequence for every patient.

Can I get a full mouth rehabilitation in one visit?

Usually not when several problems are involved. Full mouth rehabilitation is generally planned in stages, and the number of visits depends on the treatments required, healing and laboratory work.

What is the difference between full mouth rehabilitation and a smile makeover?

A smile makeover is mainly concerned with appearance. Full mouth rehabilitation is broader: it addresses dental health and function as well as appearance when multiple problems need coordinated treatment.

What happens if I have old crowns, bridges or previous dental work?

Existing restorations are assessed as part of the overall examination. Some may be usable and maintained; others may need repair or replacement depending on their condition and the health of the teeth underneath.

What are the treatment options in full mouth rehabilitation?

A plan can involve different treatments depending on which teeth can be saved and what needs to be restored. Options may include fillings, root canal treatment, crowns, bridges, dentures or implants where appropriate.
