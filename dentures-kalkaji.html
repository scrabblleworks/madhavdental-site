Dentures

A practical way to replace missing teeth when a removable solution makes sense.

Dentures are removable replacements for missing teeth. They can replace several teeth or, when all the teeth in an arch are missing, a complete set can be made.

At Madhav Dental Clinic, the starting point is not simply choosing a denture. The mouth is assessed first so that the replacement fits the situation, the remaining teeth are considered and other options can be discussed where appropriate.

01

Assess what remains

Remaining teeth, gums, supporting tissues and areas of tooth loss are considered before a replacement is planned.

02

Choose the right type

Partial or complete dentures may be considered depending on how many teeth are missing and what support is available.

03

Fit and adjust

A denture is made to the individual mouth, then checked and adjusted as needed for comfort and function.

Which type?

Partial or complete — it depends on the pattern of tooth loss.

There is no single denture that suits every patient.

Partial dentures

Used when some natural teeth remain. The denture replaces missing teeth while working alongside the teeth that are still present.

Complete dentures

Used when all teeth in an upper or lower arch are missing and a removable replacement is appropriate.

Other options

For some patients, a bridge or dental implant may be considered instead of, or in addition to, a removable denture.

What to expect

Getting a denture is a process, not a single appointment.

Examination

The mouth and remaining teeth are assessed and the options are discussed.

Records

Measurements, impressions or scans may be taken so the denture can be made for your mouth.

Fitting

The finished denture is checked in the mouth and adjusted where necessary.

Adjustment

New dentures can take time to get used to. Follow-up helps address fit or comfort problems.

Common Questions

What patients usually want to know

What are dentures?

They are removable dental appliances used to replace missing teeth. Partial dentures replace some missing teeth; complete dentures replace all teeth in an upper or lower arch.

Are dentures suitable for everyone?

They can be suitable for many people, but the right option depends on the condition of the mouth, remaining teeth, gums, jaw and your needs. An examination is needed before deciding.

What is the difference between partial and complete dentures?

Partial dentures replace some missing teeth when other natural teeth remain. Complete dentures replace all the teeth in an upper or lower arch.

How long does it take to get dentures?

The time varies because dentures are made to fit the individual mouth and may require records, laboratory work and fitting appointments. The likely sequence can be explained after examination.

Do dentures feel strange at first?

It is common to need some time to get used to new dentures. Speaking and eating can feel different initially, and follow-up adjustments may be needed.

Can dentures become loose?

They can. The supporting tissues can change over time and dentures can wear. If a denture slips, clicks or causes soreness, it should be checked.

How do I look after dentures?

Clean them regularly and handle them carefully. Remaining natural teeth, gums and tongue also need daily cleaning. Your dentist can give instructions for your specific denture.

Are dentures the only option for missing teeth?

No. Depending on the situation, bridges or dental implants may also be considered. The suitable choice depends on the teeth and tissues available and the overall plan.

Are dentures painful to wear?

New dentures can feel unfamiliar at first, and some areas may need adjustment. Persistent pain, rubbing or soreness should be checked so the fit can be corrected.

How much do dentures cost?

The cost depends on whether the denture is partial or complete, the materials used and the complexity of the case. The clinic can explain the options after examination.

How many visits are needed for dentures?

Dentures usually involve an examination, impressions or scans, laboratory work and fitting. The exact number of visits depends on the type of denture and how much adjustment is needed.
