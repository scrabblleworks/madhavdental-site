Dental Crowns & Bridges

Restoring a damaged tooth or replacing a missing one starts with understanding what is still healthy, what needs protection and which restoration is right for the tooth.

The starting point

Protect what can be preserved

A crown is used to restore and protect a damaged or weakened tooth. A bridge can replace a missing tooth by using supporting teeth or, in selected cases, implants. The right choice is not made from the name of the restoration alone — it depends on the condition of the teeth, the bite and the amount of healthy structure available.

WHEN IT MAY BE NEEDED

Common situations

A crown may be considered after extensive decay, a large filling, a fracture or root canal treatment when the tooth needs additional protection. A bridge may be considered when one or more teeth are missing and the surrounding teeth and supporting tissues are suitable.

Crown or bridge?

They solve different problems

The important distinction is simple: a crown restores a tooth that is still present; a bridge is one way of replacing a missing tooth or teeth.

01

Dental crown

A crown fits over a prepared tooth to restore its shape, strength and function. It may also be used over an implant-supported restoration in appropriate cases.

02

Dental bridge

A bridge fills a gap left by a missing tooth or teeth. The design depends on the gap, the neighbouring teeth, the supporting tissues and the overall treatment plan.

What treatment involves

From assessment to final restoration

The exact number of visits and sequence varies by case. The aim is to make the restoration fit the tooth, function well and look appropriate for the mouth.

01

Assess

The tooth, surrounding tissues, bite and available tooth structure are examined. Any treatment needed before the crown or bridge is planned first.

02

Prepare

Where appropriate, the tooth is prepared to make space for the restoration. A damaged tooth may need a build-up before it can support a crown.

03

Make

The restoration is made to the planned shape and material. A temporary restoration may be used while the final one is being prepared.

04

Fit & review

The final restoration is checked for fit, bite, appearance and comfort before it is fixed in place. Follow-up and good home care help protect the result.

Common Questions

Crowns and bridges, explained plainly

These are the questions worth answering before deciding on a restoration.

Does every root canal need a crown?

No. The decision depends on the tooth and how much sound structure remains. Teeth that have had root canal treatment can sometimes need additional protection, particularly where substantial structure has been lost.

How long does a crown last?

There is no guaranteed lifespan. The tooth underneath, the fit of the restoration, bite forces, oral hygiene and regular dental care all affect how long it remains serviceable.

Which crown material is best?

There is no universal best material. Zirconia, all-ceramic and metal-ceramic options each have different strengths and aesthetic characteristics. The material should suit the tooth and the clinical need.

Can a broken tooth be saved with a crown?

Sometimes. It depends on where the fracture is, how much healthy tooth remains and whether the tooth can be restored reliably. A broken tooth should be assessed rather than assumed to need extraction.

Is getting a crown painful?

The tooth is normally treated with local anaesthetic during preparation. Some sensitivity or gum tenderness can occur afterwards, but persistent or severe pain should be discussed with the dentist.

How much do crowns and bridges cost?

There is no useful single price without knowing the number of teeth, material and any treatment needed beforehand. The clinic can give you a clearer estimate once the teeth have been examined and the treatment plan is known.

How many visits does a crown or bridge take?

It depends on the tooth, the preparation required and the type of restoration. Some cases need more than one appointment because the final crown or bridge is made to fit the individual mouth.

What are the alternatives to a crown or bridge?

The alternatives depend on the tooth and how much healthy structure remains. In some cases a filling or other restoration may be appropriate; for a missing tooth, an implant or removable option may also be considered.
